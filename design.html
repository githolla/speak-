Design

It hovers.

Speak floats above its charging base on a centering magnetic field — always charged, always ready. And when it knows you're speaking to it, it turns to face you.

Levitation

Held by an invisible field.

Speak hovers and aligns using a centering magnetic field. Energy transfers wirelessly through resonance, so the device is always charged and always ready — no cable, no contacts, no docking ritual.

Lift it off and carry it with you. Set it back anywhere near the base and it finds its own center, settling into a quiet hover.

Presence

It turns to face you.

Speak knows when it's being addressed — through conversational context, voice direction, and proximity. The moment it does, it turns its camera toward you, the way a person turns when you say their name.

Not staring. Not recording. Just presence — and when the conversation ends, it turns away again.

Privacy

Personal without becoming communal.

Shared hardware doesn't mean shared data. The physical environment becomes part of the privacy model.

Ask: “What did I talk about with ChatGPT earlier?” Speak knows whether that conversation belongs in the room.

For sensitive information: “I can answer that privately on your phone.”

Whisper.

You can talk quietly. Speak can answer quietly. At night:

“What time is it?” “2:14.”

No glowing screen. No waking everyone up.

Private mode.

Touch and hold. The microphones physically disconnect. The light changes. Everyone in the room can see that Speak isn't listening.

Privacy shouldn't require trusting an icon buried in settings. It should be visible.

Talk privately.

“Send this to my phone.”

A conversation can seamlessly move from the room to a personal device. Sensitive subjects don't need to be spoken aloud just because the interaction began with Speak.

You decide what Speak remembers, what it forgets, and what remains private.

The hardware disappears.
The intelligence remains.
