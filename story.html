The Story

Built in an evening.

How a dinner-table conversation became this website — and what that says about the moment we're living in.

It started at dinner.

My wife and I were out with friends, and somewhere between courses the conversation drifted — the way it always does now — to AI. Someone brought up the rumor everyone seems to have heard a version of: that OpenAI is building a device. Not an app. Not another chatbot window. A physical thing that lives in your home.

Nobody at the table knew what it actually was. That was the fun of it. So we did what you do at a good dinner — we imagined it. What would it look like? Would you talk to it like a person? Would it remember things? Would it finally be the computer that doesn't feel like a computer?

The question followed me home:
what would it be like to live with it?

When we got home, my wife settled onto the couch and put on HGTV. And I couldn't let the question go.

So I sat down at the other end of the couch, opened a laptop, and decided to find out — not by reading more rumors, but by building the thing I was imagining. Not the hardware, obviously. The experience of it. What it would promise. How it would fit into a kitchen, a family calendar, a Saturday morning. What its website would say the day it launched.

One evening later, this site existed. The device on these pages — the hover, the ring of light, the coastal table it sits on — none of it is real. It's a guess. A love letter to a product that doesn't exist yet, written the same night it was imagined.

The part that gets me

Envisioning the future, in a whole new way.

Here's the strange loop I can't stop thinking about: I spent the evening imagining what it will be like to live with AI — by living with AI.

I described what I saw in my head, and it took shape on the screen while HGTV played in the background. A dinner conversation became a design. A design became a website with nine sections of product story, photography, and a working newsletter — before bedtime.

Not long ago, envisioning the future meant sketches on a napkin and a "wouldn't it be cool if." Now the distance between imagining a thing and standing inside a rendering of it is a single evening on the couch. What a neat time to envision the future.

Whatever the real device turns out to be — I hope it feels like this.

— Barry, chatgptspeak.com
