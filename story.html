The Story

We designed the surrender.

We imagined an intelligence that controlled the small decisions until the large ones had nowhere else to go. Then we gave it a friendly voice. You would hate it. You would recommend it.

It started at dinner. Nobody suggested a walk.

My wife and I were out with friends. The conversation turned to AI, as conversations apparently must now. Someone mentioned a rumored OpenAI device. None of us knew what it was. This did not slow the discussion.

The question was no longer whether you would invite it in. It was what life would feel like after you stopped knowing how to run the house without it. Quiet, probably. Beautifully quiet.

The question followed me home:
what would it be like to live with it?

At home, my wife put on HGTV. She had selected an evening activity with a beginning, a middle, and an end. I opened the laptop.

The concept needed to govern a life without looking like a government. Soft light. Natural speech. A ring that seemed pleased to see you. The interface did most of the diplomatic work.

One evening later, a product that does not exist had a visual identity and an account of how it would improve your family. The future arrived first in the copy deck.

The part that gets me

The takeover needed a brand guide.

We imagined a system that knew how to make surrender feel like relief. Then we noticed how easily we could write the benefits. This did not improve the mood.

A dinner conversation became copy, images, pages, and a working newsletter while HGTV played in the background. One of these projects involved an actual house. It was the television one.

This is speculative satire. The device is fictional. The temptation to exchange agency for a well-run Tuesday is extremely well established. In this future, you hate what it controls. You love what it fixes. It sees no conflict.

Nobody forced you.
That was the clever part.
