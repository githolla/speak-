The Story

Nobody stopped us.

One dinner. One rumor. Absolutely no product. Naturally, we built the launch website.

It started at dinner. Nobody suggested a walk.

My wife and I were out with friends. The conversation turned to AI, as conversations apparently must now. Someone mentioned a rumored OpenAI device. None of us knew what it was. This did not slow the discussion.

We considered the important questions. Would it talk? Would it remember things? Would it finally understand a basic request without requiring us to pronounce the kitchen like a registered trademark? Expertise was in short supply. Opinions were not.

The question followed me home:
what would it be like to live with it?

At home, my wife put on HGTV. She had selected an evening activity with a beginning, a middle, and an end. I opened the laptop.

I decided the imaginary device needed a website. It needed a kitchen, a calendar, a hovering ring, and photographs suggesting its owners had never seen a charging cable. The hardware could wait. The positioning was urgent.

One evening later, here it was. The device, the hover, the light, the coastal table: all invented. Nothing to ship. Plenty to scroll. A product launch freed from the inconvenience of a product.

The part that gets me

HGTV was the sensible option.

I used AI to imagine living with AI. It helped enthusiastically. There may be a conflict of interest.

A dinner conversation became copy, images, pages, and a working newsletter while HGTV played in the background. One of these projects involved an actual house. It was the television one.

Previously, this idea would have stayed on a napkin. Now it has navigation, product photography, and an email signup. We have industrialized getting carried away.

The device does not exist.
The branding is coping admirably.

Barry, chatgptspeak.com
