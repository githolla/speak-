ChatGPT Speak

Intelligence that lives with you.

ChatGPT Speak isn't a smart speaker. It's ChatGPT given a place in the physical world. Speak understands conversation, remembers what matters, works across the services you already use, coordinates the devices around you, and can take action on your behalf.

You don't need to stop what you're doing, find your phone, open an app, or formulate the perfect command.
Just speak.

Conversation

Just talk.

No commands to memorize. No rigid phrases. No menus. Speak understands normal conversation, including follow-up questions, interruptions, corrections, references to things you discussed earlier, and conversations involving multiple people.

“Do we have anything going on Saturday?” “What was the name of that restaurant Sarah liked?” “Actually, move that to Sunday.” “Remind me what we decided about the basement.” “Can you handle that?”

Speak understands the conversation around the request, not just the last sentence you said.

Presence

It knows when you're talking to it.

You don't have to begin every interaction with a wake phrase. Speak can recognize when it's being addressed through conversational context, voice direction, proximity, and interaction history.

Ask it directly.

Look toward it and speak.

Tap the surface to bring it into a conversation.

Or use a wake phrase whenever you prefer.

When Speak isn't part of the conversation, it stays out of it.

People

One ChatGPT for the household.

Speak understands that a home isn't one person. It recognizes different voices and maintains separate relationships with the people who use it.

Your preferences remain yours. Your calendar remains yours. Your messages remain yours. Shared information can become part of the household. Private information stays private.

“When is my soccer game?”
A child asking gets a different context…
“What do I have tomorrow?”
…than a parent asking.

Same Speak. Different people. Different permissions.

Action

It can actually do things.

Answers are only the beginning. Speak can work across connected services to complete tasks for you.

“Move my haircut to next week.” “Order another bag of the dog food.” “Find someone who can clean the gutters.” “Tell everyone dinner is at six.” “Book the same hotel we stayed at last time.” “Pay the electric bill.” “Find a plumber who can come tomorrow.” “Cancel that subscription.” “Send Mike the pictures from yesterday.” “Add everything we need for tacos to the grocery order.”

Speak can research, compare, communicate, schedule, organize, purchase, and coordinate. You choose which actions require approval.

Agency

Give it a job.

Some things don't need to happen immediately. Tell Speak what you want accomplished and let it work on the problem.

Speak can contact providers, gather estimates, compare reviews, check availability, ask follow-up questions, and return when there's a decision to make.

A conversation can become a task. A task can continue for hours or days. You don't have to manage every step.

“Find someone who can repair the window for under $500.”
I found three options. The best-reviewed company can come Thursday morning for $425. Want me to book it?
Awareness

Speak notices things.

With supported devices and permissions, Speak can understand what's happening around your home.

“The washer finished.” “Someone left the garage door open.” “The refrigerator door has been open for five minutes.” “The smoke detector upstairs is chirping.” “Your package was delivered.” “The basement humidity has been climbing.” “The dog has been waiting by the back door.” “You normally leave for school by now.”

But awareness doesn't have to mean interruption. Speak learns what's important enough to tell you now, what can wait, and what doesn't require your attention at all.

It knows when not to talk.

Good intelligence isn't constantly interrupting you. Speak can communicate without speaking.

A soft pulse can mean there's something waiting. A different light can indicate an active task. Touch the surface when you're ready.

Not twenty notifications. Not another inbox. Just what matters.

“What did I miss?”
And Speak gives you the important things.

Everyday life

Part of the house, not another device in it.

Cook with it.

Speak can become part of the kitchen without requiring you to touch a screen.

“What can I make with what we have?” “Walk me through the risotto.” “How much is two-thirds of a cup doubled?” “Start a timer when I put these in.” “Is this supposed to look this thick?” “What did I change last time?” “Add parmesan to the grocery list.”

And your recipes can evolve with you.

“Save this version. Everyone liked it better.”

Next time: “Make the pasta we liked.” Speak knows which one.

Shop naturally.

You shouldn't need to remember everything you need while standing in a grocery aisle. Just mention things as they happen.

“We're almost out of coffee.” “We need toothpaste.” “Don't buy those paper towels again.” “Get the cereal the kids actually ate.”

Speak can maintain a living household list and, when you allow it, build or place orders automatically. Before checkout:

“You have 17 items. I found the same detergent for $6 less in the larger size. Switch it?”

Plan dinner.

“People are coming over Friday.”

Speak can help turn that sentence into everything that follows — suggest a menu based on dietary preferences, create the grocery list, check what you already have, schedule grocery delivery, remind you when food needs to start cooking, adjust recipes for eight people, and coordinate timing.

“When should I put the roast in?” “5:10 if you want to eat at seven.”

Remember your recipes.

Speak doesn't just know recipes from the internet. It can know your recipes.

“Mom uses more cinnamon.” “Dad doesn't like onions.” “We doubled the sauce last time.” “That took 45 minutes, not 30.” “Use the version we made at Christmas.”

Over time, your family's cooking becomes part of your household knowledge.

Plan trips together.

Travel planning becomes a conversation. Speak can research destinations, understand everyone's schedules, compare flights, evaluate hotels, consider weather, account for preferences, and build complete options.

“We need somewhere warm.” “How long?” “Maybe four nights.” “Beach, resort, or somewhere with things to do?” “Beach, but not just sitting around.”

Then: “Option two.” Flights, hotel, transportation, reservations, calendar events, packing reminders, and itinerary can all follow from the decision.

Remember where you've been.

“What was that restaurant near our hotel in Chicago?”

Speak can connect past trips, calendars, photos, reservations, messages, and household memory.

“The Italian place was Monteverde. You went Saturday night and later said the burrata was the best thing you ordered.” “Can we go again?” “I found a table Friday at 7:30.”

Manage the family calendar.

Speak doesn't just display calendars. It understands the people behind them.

“Find a night everyone is free.” “Can Emma make the dentist Tuesday?” “When can we visit Mom?” “Move dinner somewhere after Jake's practice.” “Give us one weekend with absolutely nothing scheduled.”

It can reason across multiple schedules while respecting everyone's permissions.

Help with homework — without doing it for them.

“I'm stuck.”

Speak can adjust how it teaches based on the person asking. Instead of immediately giving the answer:

“Show me where you're stuck.”

It can explain concepts differently, generate examples, ask questions, quiz students, create study plans, and remember what they've mastered.

“Quiz me on the words I missed yesterday.”

Speak already knows which ones.

Read together.

“Keep reading.”

Speak remembers where you stopped. Ask questions while listening.

“Why did he do that?” “Who is she again?” “Was that mentioned earlier?” “What does that word mean?”

The book becomes conversational without losing your place.

Tell stories.

“Tell us a story about a dragon named Pickles.”

Speak can create stories interactively.

“What should Pickles find?” “A spaceship!” “And who's flying it?” “Grandma!”

The story changes as everyone participates. Tomorrow: “Continue the Pickles story.” It remembers where you left off.

Capture family stories.

“Tell Speak the story about Grandpa's first car.”

Speak can listen, organize the story, identify people and places, and preserve it in your family's private archive. Later, someone can ask:

“What did Grandpa say his first job was?”

Family history becomes searchable.

Help care for the people you love.

Speak can provide gentle support for routines when people choose to use it.

Connected households can share selected information without sharing everything.

Privacy

Personal without becoming communal.

Shared hardware doesn't mean shared data. Ask: “What did I talk about with ChatGPT earlier?” Speak knows whether that conversation belongs in the room.

For sensitive information: “I can answer that privately on your phone.” The physical environment becomes part of the privacy model.

Whisper.

You can talk quietly. Speak can answer quietly. At night:

“What time is it?” “2:14.”

No glowing screen. No waking everyone up.

Private mode.

Touch and hold. The microphones physically disconnect. The light changes. Everyone in the room can see that Speak isn't listening.

Privacy shouldn't require trusting an icon buried in settings. It should be visible.

Talk privately.

One presence

One intelligence. Every room.

Multiple Speak devices don't behave like separate assistants. They form a single presence. Start a conversation in the kitchen. Continue in the hallway. Finish upstairs. Your context follows you.

The hardware disappears. The intelligence remains.

Speak understands interruption.

“Remind me to—” “Dad!” “Hang on.”

Twenty seconds later:

“What was I saying?” “You were asking me to remind you about something.” “Oh yeah. Call Mark tomorrow.”

Human conversation is messy. Speak is designed for that.

It understands “that.”

“Order that again.” “Play that song.” “Send that to Sarah.” “Put that on my calendar.” “Remember that.”

The most powerful interface might be pronouns. Because context makes them work.

It understands “later.”

“Remind me later.”

Speak can reason about what later means. If you're driving, later might mean when you arrive. If you're cooking, later might mean after dinner. If something has a deadline, Speak can make sure “later” doesn't become “forgotten.”

It understands intent.

“I'm exhausted.”

That doesn't automatically trigger an action. Maybe Speak says:

“You've got nothing else scheduled tonight.”

Or perhaps:

“Want me to move your morning workout?”

Intelligence means understanding when a statement is just a statement.

It can disagree.

“Should I buy this?”

Speak doesn't have to validate the idea.

“You've looked at three versions of that camera, but based on how you actually use your current one, I don't think the upgrade gives you much.”

A useful assistant shouldn't always say yes.

Ask it to challenge you.

“Tell me why this is a bad idea.” “Argue the other side.” “What am I missing?” “What assumptions am I making?” “Would you spend the money?”

Speak can become friction when friction is useful.

Philosophy

Ask less.

The evolution of computing has mostly required humans to learn how computers want to be used. Speak reverses that relationship.

You shouldn't have to know which application controls the thermostat. Which calendar contains the appointment. Which store sold the product. Where the receipt lives. Which streaming service has the movie. Which person sent the message.

You tell Speak what you're trying to accomplish. It figures out the rest.

And perhaps the most important: “Thanks.” Not because Speak needs it. Because eventually, talking to intelligence starts feeling less like operating a computer and more like sharing a space with one.
