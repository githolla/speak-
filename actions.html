Actions

It can actually do things.

Answers are only the beginning. Speak can work across connected services to complete tasks for you.

“Move my haircut to next week.” “Order another bag of the dog food.” “Find someone who can clean the gutters.” “Tell everyone dinner is at six.” “Book the same hotel we stayed at last time.” “Pay the electric bill.” “Find a plumber who can come tomorrow.” “Cancel that subscription.” “Send Mike the pictures from yesterday.” “Add everything we need for tacos to the grocery order.”

Speak can research, compare, communicate, schedule, organize, purchase, and coordinate. You choose which actions require approval.

Agency

Give it a job.

Some things don't need to happen immediately. Tell Speak what you want accomplished and let it work on the problem.

Speak can contact providers, gather estimates, compare reviews, check availability, ask follow-up questions, and return when there's a decision to make.

A conversation can become a task. A task can continue for hours or days. You don't have to manage every step.

“Find someone who can repair the window for under $500.”
I found three options. The best-reviewed company can come Thursday morning for $425. Want me to book it?

Handle the boring stuff.

“Call the insurance company and figure out why the bill increased.” “Get the internet company to lower our rate.” “Cancel the trial before it renews.” “Find out where that refund is.” “Schedule the inspection.” “Return these shoes.”

Speak can deploy agents to deal with administrative work and return when your judgment or authorization is needed.

Wait on hold for me.

Nobody should spend forty minutes listening to hold music. Speak can handle supported service interactions and bring you in when a human actually needs you.

“They're ready for you.”

Research while you live your life.

“Figure out which lawn mower we should buy.”

Speak doesn't need you standing there while it researches. Later:

“I narrowed it down to three. One is cheaper, one is quieter, and one has significantly better repairability. Based on what you've told me, I'd pick the quieter one.”

You can ask “Why?” and continue from there.

Watch something for me.

“Tell me when these tickets go on sale.” “Let me know when that package arrives.” “Watch the price of that flight.” “Tell me when the repair person is close.” “Let me know if tomorrow's game gets moved.”

Some requests aren't questions. They're things you want another intelligence to keep track of.

Make decisions together.

Speak doesn't need to decide everything. It can reduce complexity until the decision becomes easy.

“We have five contractors. Two can't come this month. One is significantly more expensive. These two are the strongest choices.”

Then you decide. The goal isn't removing humans from decisions. It's removing the work required to reach them.

Catch me up.

One of Speak's most common requests can simply be: “Catch me up.” It understands the context.

Work: “You received two messages that need decisions.”

Home: “The plumber confirmed tomorrow and your grocery order substituted two items.”

Family: “Emma's practice moved to six.”

You don't need to check every system separately.

Work

Think out loud.

Some thinking works better when you're walking around. Speak becomes a thinking partner — and the computer becomes available wherever you are.

Thinking

Brainstorm out loud.

“I'm trying to figure something out.” Speak can become a thinking partner — challenge an idea, organize thoughts, remember unfinished ideas, connect something you said earlier.

Your spoken thoughts become usable knowledge instead of disappearing.

Wait. That's related to the thing you mentioned Tuesday about customer onboarding.

Capture ideas instantly.

“I just had an idea.” Say it. That's enough. Speak can save it, categorize it, connect it to a project, expand it later, or remind you when it's relevant.

“Show me the ideas I've had about the new product.”
Your shower thoughts finally have a database.

Meetings without meeting notes.

Put Speak in a room with permission from everyone participating. It can capture decisions rather than simply producing transcripts. Afterward:

“We made three decisions, assigned four actions, and left two questions unresolved.” “Why did we decide against option B?”

Speak can retrieve the reasoning behind the decision.

Work without sitting at your computer.

“What's happening with the Anderson project?” “Anything I need to respond to?” “Give me the important stuff from Slack.” “Draft a response to Kelly.” “Read it.” “Make that less formal.” “Send it.”

The computer becomes available while you're walking, cooking, getting dressed, or staring at a whiteboard.
