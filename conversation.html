Conversation

Just talk.

No commands to memorize. No rigid phrases. No menus. Speak understands normal conversation, including follow-up questions, interruptions, corrections, references to things you discussed earlier, and conversations involving multiple people.

“Do we have anything going on Saturday?” “What was the name of that restaurant Sarah liked?” “Actually, move that to Sunday.” “Remind me what we decided about the basement.” “Can you handle that?”

Speak understands the conversation around the request, not just the last sentence you said.

Presence

It knows when you're talking to it.

You don't have to begin every interaction with a wake phrase. Speak can recognize when it's being addressed through conversational context, voice direction, proximity, and interaction history.

Ask it directly.

Look toward it and speak.

Tap the surface to bring it into a conversation.

Or use a wake phrase whenever you prefer.

When Speak isn't part of the conversation, it stays out of it.

Designed for real speech

Human conversation is messy.
Speak is designed for that.

Speak understands interruption.

“Remind me to—” “Dad!” “Hang on.”

Twenty seconds later:

“What was I saying?” “You were asking me to remind you about something.” “Oh yeah. Call Mark tomorrow.”

It understands “that.”

“Order that again.” “Play that song.” “Send that to Sarah.” “Put that on my calendar.” “Remember that.”

The most powerful interface might be pronouns. Because context makes them work.

It understands “later.”

“Remind me later.”

Speak can reason about what later means. If you're driving, later might mean when you arrive. If you're cooking, later might mean after dinner. If something has a deadline, Speak can make sure “later” doesn't become “forgotten.”

It understands intent.

“I'm exhausted.”

That doesn't automatically trigger an action. Maybe Speak says:

“You've got nothing else scheduled tonight.”

Or perhaps:

“Want me to move your morning workout?”

Intelligence means understanding when a statement is just a statement.

It can disagree.

“Should I buy this?”

Speak doesn't have to validate the idea.

“You've looked at three versions of that camera, but based on how you actually use your current one, I don't think the upgrade gives you much.”

A useful assistant shouldn't always say yes.

Ask it to challenge you.

“Tell me why this is a bad idea.” “Argue the other side.” “What am I missing?” “What assumptions am I making?” “Would you spend the money?”

Speak can become friction when friction is useful.

Translate conversations.

“Translate between me and Maria.”

Speak can become a natural interpreter. Each person speaks normally. Speak preserves meaning, tone and context instead of translating isolated sentences.

Learn a language by living with it.

“Only answer me in Spanish during breakfast.”

Speak can gently correct mistakes, introduce vocabulary, remember what you're learning, and adjust difficulty over time. Language learning becomes part of everyday life.

Practice anything.

“Interview me.” “Run through my presentation.” “Quiz me.” “Pretend you're the customer.” “Challenge my argument.” “Don't let me use filler words.”

Speak turns an empty room into a practice environment.

Settle arguments.

“What movie was that?” “How far is the moon?” “Was that actually illegal?” “Who played first base?” “What temperature should chicken be?”

The world's most overqualified dinner-table referee is sitting six feet away. But it can also say “I don't know.” Speak distinguishes between what it knows, what it remembers, what it can verify, and what it's uncertain about. Ask it to check — it can research the question and come back with sources.

Memory

A memory for your home.

Speak can remember the things that disappear into everyday life.

Where you put something What you decided Which contractor fixed the roof The paint color in the bedroom Your daughter's favorite restaurant The instructions for resetting the furnace What everyone wanted for Christmas The name of the wine you loved on vacation The story your grandfather told at dinner

You decide what Speak remembers, what it forgets, and what remains private.

Ask:

“Where did I put the passports?” “What size filter does the furnace take?” “Who installed the dishwasher?” “What did we decide about Thanksgiving?” “What was that hotel we stayed at in Maine?”

Speak searches your household memory and gives you the answer.

People

One ChatGPT for the household.

Speak understands that a home isn't one person. It recognizes different voices and maintains separate relationships with the people who use it.

Your preferences remain yours. Your calendar remains yours. Your messages remain yours. Shared information can become part of the household. Private information stays private.

“When is my soccer game?”
A child asking gets a different context…
“What do I have tomorrow?”
…than a parent asking.

Same Speak. Different people. Different permissions.

Your world

Ask about your own life.

Traditional AI knows the world. Speak can also understand the parts of your world you've chosen to connect.

“When did we replace the water heater?” “How much did we pay?” “Who did it?” “Is it still under warranty?” “What did they say about maintenance?”

The answers can come from receipts, email, documents, calendars, conversations and household memory.

People, not records

Understand relationships.

“Invite my brother.” “Send that to Dad.” “Ask our neighbor if she wants one.”

Speak understands people as people, not contact records. And it knows that “Mom” means something different depending on who's speaking.

Continuity

Give your home a memory.

Most homes forget everything. Speak doesn't have to.

“What's the model number of the dishwasher?” “Do we still have the receipt?” “When should the HVAC be serviced again?”

Your home becomes searchable.

When you move

Years of knowledge, transferable.

“Prepare the house information for the buyers.”
Speak can assemble manuals, improvements, service history, warranties, paint colors, smart-home instructions and maintenance records.

Years of household knowledge become transferable instead of disappearing.

In real homes

Things people say to Speak

“What's going on today?” “Handle it.” “Find a better one.” “Where did I put that?” “Did anyone respond?” “Order what we usually get.” “Explain that.” “Keep going.” “Remember this.” “Don't let me forget.” “Tell everyone.” “Figure it out.” “Check first.” “Only tell me if it matters.” “Make the house comfortable.” “Get everything ready.” “What am I forgetting?” “What do you think?” “Thanks.”

And perhaps the most important: “Thanks.” Not because Speak needs it. Because eventually, talking to intelligence starts feeling less like operating a computer and more like sharing a space with one.
