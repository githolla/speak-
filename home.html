The Home

Your home can talk to itself.

Speak can coordinate connected devices instead of making you control them individually.

“It's movie night.”
Lights adjust. Blinds close. The television turns on. Temperature changes. Phones quiet down.
Want me to order the pizza too?

Don't automate devices.
Automate outcomes.

Instead of
“Set the thermostat to 70.”
Try
“It's cold in here.”
Instead of
“Turn off the downstairs lights.”
Try
“Everyone's going to bed.”
Instead of
“Start the robot vacuum.”
Try
“Get the house ready before people arrive.”

Speak determines what needs to happen across connected devices.

Routines

Routines without programming them.

You don't build automations. You describe what you want. Speak turns intentions into routines.

“When the last person leaves, shut everything down.” “On school mornings, tell us if we're running late.” “If nobody's downstairs after midnight, turn everything off.” “Keep the bedroom cooler when we're sleeping.” “Don't run the vacuum when the baby's asleep.”
Music

Music becomes conversational.

Speak can understand the room, occasion, people present, and listening history. Music doesn't need to begin with knowing the name of a playlist.

“Play something relaxed.”
“More upbeat.”
“Not that upbeat.”
“Something like what we listened to Saturday.”
“Everyone likes this. Save it.”
Whole home

Sound follows you.

With multiple Speak devices, audio can move naturally through the home.

“Keep playing upstairs.” “Move this to the kitchen.” “Play this everywhere.”

Or Speak can infer movement when you've enabled it. Walk from the kitchen into the living room and your podcast continues there.

Continuity

Have a conversation from anywhere.

Start downstairs: “Help me think through the presentation tomorrow.” Continue while walking upstairs. Speak maintains the conversation across devices.

You aren't talking to individual speakers. You're talking to the same ChatGPT throughout the home.

One presence

One intelligence. Every room.

Multiple Speak devices don't behave like separate assistants. They form a single presence. Start a conversation in the kitchen. Continue in the hallway. Finish upstairs. Your context follows you.

The hardware disappears. The intelligence remains.

The room

Understand the room.

With compatible sensors and explicit permission, Speak can understand more than sound — who is present, whether someone entered, ambient temperature, noise level, connected-device status, environmental conditions.

That means these become meaningful requests without specifying devices:

“Turn it down.” “It's too bright.” “What's making that noise?” “Is something burning?” “Why is it so hot upstairs?”
Space

Know which room you're talking about.

“Turn the lights off.” You shouldn't need to say “Turn the dining-room ceiling lights off.”

Speak knows where the request originated and what you're probably referring to. Language becomes spatial.

“Where are my keys?”
You left them on the entry table.
“What about my glasses?”
They were last seen upstairs near the bed.

Connected tags, devices, cameras where permitted, and household memory can work together.

Listening

Recognize sounds.

Speak can identify important household sounds.

Breaking glass Smoke alarms Water running unexpectedly A crying baby A barking dog An appliance alarm A doorbell A possible leak

You choose which sounds matter and what Speak should do when it hears them.

Awareness

Speak notices things.

With supported devices and permissions, Speak can understand what's happening around your home.

“The washer finished.” “Someone left the garage door open.” “The refrigerator door has been open for five minutes.” “The smoke detector upstairs is chirping.” “Your package was delivered.” “The basement humidity has been climbing.” “The dog has been waiting by the back door.” “You normally leave for school by now.”

But awareness doesn't have to mean interruption. Speak learns what's important enough to tell you now, what can wait, and what doesn't require your attention at all.

Restraint

It knows when not to talk.

Good intelligence isn't constantly interrupting you. Speak can communicate without speaking.

A soft pulse can mean there's something waiting. A different light can indicate an active task. Touch the surface when you're ready.

Not twenty notifications. Not another inbox. Just what matters.

“What did I miss?”
And Speak gives you the important things.
Anticipation

Help before you ask.

Speak can notice when information becomes useful.

You're getting ready to leave — the highway is backed up. Leaving now through Route 30 saves about twelve minutes.
The oven is preheated.
Your passport isn't with the things you've packed.
The garage is still open.

Proactive doesn't mean intrusive. You control how often Speak speaks first.

Judgment

A different kind of notification.

Phones turned everything into notifications. Speak can turn notifications back into judgment.

Twenty-seven alerts might become:

Nothing urgent. There are three things you'll probably want to look at later.
One thing needs you now.
“What was that thing you told me this morning?”
The school delayed opening because of weather.
“No, after that.”
The plumber moved the appointment.
“That's it.”

Conversation becomes navigable.

Away

Let it run the house while you're away.

“We're leaving for a week.” Speak can activate an away routine — adjust climate, manage lights, watch supported sensors, pause unnecessary routines, handle deliveries, coordinate pet care, and notify you only when something actually needs attention.

Return

Come home to a house that's ready.

“We land at six.” Speak can coordinate the home around your arrival. Temperature returns to normal. Lights come on if it's dark. Groceries can arrive. The robot vacuum finishes before you get home.

Your evening doesn't begin with resetting the house.
